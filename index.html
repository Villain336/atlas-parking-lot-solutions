ADA / Handicap Markings: What Matters

ADA stalls, access aisles, and signage need proper layout and visibility. We mark clean, code-aligned spaces that help keep your lot accessible and reduce risk. Each handicap space must be at least 8 feet wide with a 5-foot access aisle, and van-accessible spaces require 11 feet of width. Proper signage, blue pavement markings, and clear crosshatching are non-negotiable for compliance and liability protection.

How Often Should You Re-Stripe?

Most commercial parking lots need restriping every 18 to 24 months depending on traffic volume, weather exposure, and paint quality. High-traffic areas like retail centers, medical offices, and schools often require annual touch-ups to maintain visibility and compliance. Faded lines create confusion, increase liability, and hurt your property's professional appearance. Regular restriping protects your investment and keeps your lot safe and code-compliant.

Fire Lanes: Simple Mistakes That Cost

Fire lanes must be clearly marked and readable. We keep markings consistent so emergency access stays obvious, and your property looks professionally maintained. Fire marshals enforce strict visibility standards, and faded or missing fire lane markings can result in fines, failed inspections, or worse, delayed emergency response. Red curbs, NO PARKING stencils, and contrasting yellow or white edge lines ensure compliance and safety.

Extend Your Pavement's Lifespan and Ensure Full Regulatory Compliance

Your pavement, like many things we own, needs consistent care to stay healthy and usable. The science behind it is surprisingly simple: asphalt seems like just a hard surface but it's flexible. The flexibility comes with wear and tear over time when enduring various conditions. UV rays, water creeping into every crack, vehicle and foot traffic, and even everyday chemicals are all working to wear it down. Over time, that relentless sun turns the asphalt's binder brittle, making the surface weak and crumbly. Meanwhile, water is the silent saboteur, finding its way into those tiny cracks and slowly undermining the very foundation beneath. Sealcoating, crack filling, and proactive maintenance protect your investment and extend pavement life by 10 to 15 years.

How long does striping paint take to dry?

Dry time varies by product and weather. Water-based paint typically dries in 30 to 60 minutes, while oil-based and thermoplastic markings may take longer. Many lots can reopen the same day, but full cure time is influenced by temperature, humidity, and surface condition. We schedule work to minimize disruption and ensure safe, durable results.

Can you add ADA-compliant parking and access aisles to an existing lot?

Yes. We can lay out handicap and van-accessible stalls, access aisles, crosshatching, and related markings so your lot is clearly marked, easier to navigate, and fully compliant with federal ADA standards and North Carolina building codes. Proper ADA striping reduces liability and ensures accessibility for all visitors.

Do you offer crack filling and sealcoating before restriping?

Yes. If cracking or surface damage is present, crack filling and sealcoating help slow water intrusion, improve structural integrity, and enhance the finished appearance before restriping. Addressing pavement issues first ensures your new striping adheres properly and lasts longer.

How long does parking lot striping last in North Carolina weather?

Quality traffic paint typically lasts 18 to 36 months depending on traffic volume, sun exposure, and maintenance. North Carolina's hot summers, freeze-thaw cycles, and heavy rain accelerate wear. High-traffic areas like entrances and drive aisles fade faster. Regular inspections and proactive restriping keep your lot safe, compliant, and looking professional year-round.
